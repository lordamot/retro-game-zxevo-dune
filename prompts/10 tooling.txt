Two tools, and the first thing they caught is already fixed.

The problem with what was there

Everything in this repository photographed the demo: a script of button
presses from power-on, a screenshot at the end. That is fine for a screen
that is always the same and useless for "what does this button do", because
the answer costs a full boot and cannot be compared with the answer before
it. My last session died on exactly that - I spent a long time driving the
demo's build menu blind and got nowhere.

nes_play.py - drive it, do not photograph it

retro-run can now save and reload the whole machine (and reset, and dump
cartridge RAM). So a mark is a moment, and every experiment after it is
load, press, look. A step is 0.7 seconds instead of a minute, and two
experiments from the same mark differ only by what was pressed.

  nes_play.py marks                            boot, battle, menu
  nes_play.py do battle "b, start"             press and dump everything
  nes_play.py watch battle "run 4, right, ..." which of the 2 KB moved
  nes_play.py find battle 1500 --u16           where a number lives
  nes_play.py goto battle 144 112              put the cursor there
  nes_play.py state battle                     what is known, decoded

It works. watch found the cursor in one run: $001A and $001C, four at a
time, and nothing else in the 2 KB moves when a direction is pressed. goto
uses that and then reads the position back rather than trusting the
arithmetic - asked for 120,96, got 120,96. KNOWN in the tool is the memory
map so far, and it says how each entry was found, so it grows by
measurement rather than by guessing.

What I did not find: the credits. 1500 is not in the work RAM as 16-bit,
BCD, digits or anything else I tried, and the cartridge has no SRAM to
look in. It is somewhere I have not thought of yet, and the tool is how
that gets settled rather than a reason to stop.

compare.py - the port beside the demo

make compare puts the two side by side, screen for screen, in
tmp/compare/, with a number beside each.

The number is not a pixel diff, because there is nothing honest to diff:
320x200 in sixteen of 64 against 256x240 in the NES's own palette, and a
port that deliberately shows more map. What can be compared is how the
colours are used - each screen reduced to its palette and the share of the
picture each colour holds, matched by colour distance. A screen that is
the same picture in the same colours scores near zero however different
its resolution; one that has drifted to the wrong palette scores badly.

  intro 0.278   title 0.351   options 1.394
  house 0.489   mentat 0.243  battle 0.279

It ranked the options screen worst on its own, which is right: the demo
shows a yellow ornithopter and the port shows a list of keys. That is item
4 on the port's own list, and the tool found it without being told.

The first fix

The battle comparison showed the port printing 01500 where the demo prints
1500. txt_num pads with zeros; there is now txt_numb beside it that blanks
the leading ones, which costs nothing because glyph 0 is the space, so the
number stays right-aligned in the same five columns. The credits and the
build costs use it; the map seed still prints padded, which is what a seed
wants.

make build and make verify pass. Nothing committed.

What the comparison says to do next, in its own order: the options screen
(1.39) is a whole picture that is captured and unused; the house screen
(0.49) and title (0.35) are next. The battle at 0.28 is mostly the sand
being more saturated in the port than in the demo, which is a palette
question the tool can now be pointed at directly.
