The EMC side is now most of the way closed, and it turned up a correction
to what I said last time.

The correction first. I had the calling convention backwards. A call site
is

    push.b  4                  the argument
    call    0
    drop    1                  the caller takes its arguments back
    push    the return value   - only if it wants the answer

and I had been looking for that push *before* the drop. Reading it the
wrong way round said 13 of the 79 routines the scripts call are questions
and the other 66 are orders. Reading it the right way round says 36 and
43. The wrong figure is the one that should have looked suspicious: it had
the twenty-property getter down as a routine whose answer nobody ever
wanted.

Then the routines themselves. The 104 call slots are 88 distinct routines,
because the three tables share some - the delay is UNIT 16, TEAM 0 and
BUILD 0. Twenty of them are moveq #0,d0 / rts and do nothing at all. Of
the rest, 48 are now named in res/data/functions.txt, read one at a time
in orig/sega/src/; the remainder were read and not settled, and the file
says so rather than putting a name on a guess.

Two things made the reading go quickly once they were found.

The three scripts differ only in which global they work on: $FFDEA0 is the
unit a UNIT.EMC script is running for, $FFD5AC the structure a BUILD.EMC
script is running for, $FFDCA8 the team, and $FFC208 whichever of those it
is - which is what the shared $011xxx routines use. So the same shape of
code means three different things depending on which table it is in.

And everything a script passes around is a tagged reference: one word, the
top two bits the kind and the low fourteen the value. The $02Exxx module
reads them - 0 is nothing, 1 a structure, 2 a unit, 3 a square of the map.
$02E2FA says whether a reference is still good, $02E256 gives its world
position, $02E1F6 its map square, $02E17C builds one. That is why a script
can say "go to that" without caring whether that is a building, a vehicle
or a patch of sand.

The vocabulary that comes out of it, for a unit: get - twenty properties
through one switch - and then act, go.to, aim, fire, turn, step, speed,
stop, die, explode, deliver, dock, launch, delay, distance, enemy?, kind,
clear?, find.idle, find.home, wander, count. For a building it is much
shorter: produce, release, animate, aim, fire, sound, target. For a team
shorter still: members, recruit, centre, find.target, attack, behave.

The fourteen orders

$0436A0 is the routine that gives a unit an order and it reads a table of
twelve-byte records. The name of order n is the longword four bytes
*before* its record - that is where the record in front ends - and getting
that wrong is what puts Move at 0 instead of Attack, which then reports
every unit in every mission file one order out. The list is Attack, Move,
Retreat, Guard, Area Guard, Harvest, Return, Stop, Ambush, Sabotage, Die,
Hunt, Deploy, Destruct.

Two independent checks say the numbering is right. Every unit type's
default order at +$28 of the unit table comes out sensible - Guard for
everything that fights, Stop for the Carryall, the Harvester, the MCV and
every projectile, and Attack for the Sandworm, which is the one thing on
Arrakis that needs no telling. And the only two orders whose record says
they may interrupt a unit that is already busy are Die and Destruct.

It is one numbering across the whole game, so missions.txt now says Hunt
and Ambush for the enemy soldiers and Area Guard for the player's, and a
script's act reads as a word.

And variable 0 of a unit's script is the order it is under. That is not a
reading of the pattern: the script object lives at unit+$16 and its
variables at +$C, so variable 0 is unit+$22, and $043766 inside $0436A0 is
move.w d3,$22(a2) with d3 the order. So a unit script is a switch on its
current order, and the twenty-seven entry points are what each kind of
unit does differently inside it.

What a listing looks like now

    w0:
        0  4404       push.b    4
        1  4E00       call    0   ; get facing
        2  4C01       drop    1
        3  4200       push    the return value
        4  4701       push.loc    1
        5  5103       binary    !=
        6  2F00 8016  goto-if    w22
        8  4E1A       call    26  ; stop
        9  4701       push.loc    1
       10  4E07       call    7   ; turn
       11  4C01       drop    1
    w12:
       12  440A       push.b    10
       13  4E00       call    0   ; get turn.left
       14  4C01       drop    1
       15  4200       push    the return value
       16  2F00 8016  goto-if    w22
       18  440A       push.b    10
       19  4E10       call    16  ; delay
       20  4C01       drop    1
       21  800C       goto    w12
    w22:
       22  4801       pop    and return

which is "if you are not facing that way, stop and turn, then wait until
you have got there" - the routine every other unit routine calls before it
does anything.
