I went at the top of the port's own list - confirming the chosen art - and
came back with a measurement rather than a fix. Four of the port's pictures
are provably not the demo's.

The check, and why it is a check

A building's picture in the demo is sixteen bytes in the program: four rows
of four tileset numbers, one 2x2 map square to each 2x2 of them. That is
not a guess. The refinery's four cells were identified off a real screen
long ago -

  cell 8,8  $10A,$10A,$160,$161      cell 9,8  $10A,$151,$162,$151
  cell 8,9  $170,$171,$150,$150      cell 9,9  $172,$151,$150,$152

- and the sixteen bytes at $02470 in the file are those same patterns read
across instead of square by square:

  0A 0A 0A 51 / 60 61 62 51 / 70 71 72 51 / 50 50 50 52

Sixteen bytes agreeing with a screen to the byte is not luck. It is also
the only check available, because the demo never draws a factory, so a
picture invented out of the atlas cannot be caught any other way.

The result

nes_rom.py --buildings now runs that lookup for every building line in
src/res/nes_art.txt, with the refinery as a control so a broken check
cannot pass silently.

  control: the refinery, read off a real screen, is in the program at $02470
  factory   is NOT in the program: invented from the atlas
  barracks  is NOT in the program: invented from the atlas
  silo      is NOT in the program: invented from the atlas
  turret    is NOT in the program: invented from the atlas

So those four are the refinery's frame with middles picked off the atlas by
eye. They are the cartridge's own pixels and they draw as buildings, but
they are not the cartridge's own buildings. nes_art.txt, progress.md,
nes-art.md and the skill all say so now, in those words.

What I tried and would not do again

Playing the demo into building one. I drove it to the battlefield, selected
a building, opened the build menu and cycled it: exactly two items at
mission start, and Left/Right alternates between them. Mission 2 starts
from the same three buildings on different ground. The demo does not build
for itself. Getting to a factory means playing several minutes of Dune II
through a blind script, and I stopped rather than spend the session there.

Matching against the Mega Drive's named sprites. Those are the 128-pixel
mentat-screen portraits, not map tiles, so there is nothing to match.

The demo's building table. The tool draws all 42 candidate pictures in
