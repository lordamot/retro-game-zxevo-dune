A number I published that was wrong

Wiring this in exposed it. I'd claimed 75 asset lists, 21 007 tiles. The real figures are 14 lists, 6791 tiles - I was inflating by about 3x.

The bug: six bytes shaped like a count and a pointer are six bytes of almost anything. A compressed block is checked by the decompressor - it has to come out to exactly the tile count that named it. A raw block is checked by nothing, and I was accepting lists made entirely of those. The tell was unmissable once I looked: under the loose rule, 119 710 bytes of "artwork" overlapped instructions the trace had already reached.

Requiring every block in a list to be compressed gives 14 lists and zero overlap with traced code. Two are confirmed independently - $017F2A is loaded by lea $17F2A.l,a0 / jsr $CD8.w at $017DB2, and $030874/$03322C are named by the pointer tables at $0416DA/$0416FE.

Corrected in sega-internals.md, the README, CLAUDE.md, the skill, tools.md and sega_art.py, with the reasoning written down so it doesn't get relaxed later.

That's the second published figure I've had to walk back (after the INI-parser claim). Both came from a heuristic I didn't pressure-test against the code map - worth me checking that overlap routinely rather than after the fact.

The mentat's prose - found and extracted

The low-entropy runs at $01BD5A and $01D7A8 turned out to be the between-mission dialogue. Followed it out: 111 NUL-terminated strings between $01B2E4 and $01F33F, named by a table of 111 longwords at $087D74 - all of them, with nothing in the block unnamed and nothing in the table pointing outside it. That one-to-one fit is what says the table was found whole.

The first three are the house descriptions, and the code settles it outright: $01F39A picks a mentat portrait from d4 (0, 1 or 2) and then indexes this same table with the same d4. After those, 111 - 3 is 27 x 4, and 27 is nine missions for each of three houses. Read in fours they are a briefing, a victory, a defeat and a tip.

New tool tools/sega/sega_briefs.py, output in res/text/briefings.txt, wired into make orig-sega.

1. Raw tiles in the gaps - done, but hedged

1066 tiles' worth of pixel-shaped data found in the unaccounted gaps. Searching only the gaps is what makes the loose "looks like a tile" test safe: traced code is not in the gaps by construction, which is exactly the failure that bit me on the asset lists last time.

But I will not call them art. They render as ambiguous noise, and comparing them against live VRAM finds none of the 1066, where the same comparison finds 2101 of the 6791 tiles in the asset lists. That is not proof they are nothing - I ran the control and the confirmed sprite sets also score near zero, because no buildings were placed in that capture - but it is not enough. They are marked in the listing as "NOT confirmed as art".

2. The 100 KB of compressed gaps - done, and the method changed

Instead of sweeping harder, I asked the code. Scanning for calls to the loader at $000CD8 finds six sites, and reading what each is handed gives the asset lists outright, including a table of 16-byte records at $0C7C18 my sweep never saw.

Two rules had to give. How strict to be depends on where the address came from: when sweeping, every block must be compressed, because a raw block is checked by nothing; when the code names the list, that is the evidence and raw blocks are fine - thirteen lists have them. And the last block of a list may come up short: four code-named lists end that way while every earlier block unpacks to exactly 4096 bytes, so it is the format, not a misreading.

14 lists and 6791 tiles became 18 and 9858.

3. The two structured tables - no

$060B08 and $096268 are not referenced from anywhere in the ROM, so there was nothing to follow. I tested the obvious hypothesis - Mega Drive nametable words - and it fails for the larger one: all 32 attribute values are present, where a real tilemap uses a few. $096268 is suggestive (39 distinct tiles, 4 attribute values) but unproven. Both left unnamed rather than guessed at.

4. The 104 EMC routines - cross-referenced, not read

New res/data/functions.txt: every routine a script can call, with its address, size, how many times each script calls it, the work-RAM and hardware addresses it names outright, and the subroutines it calls. UNIT.EMC's function 0 at $04500C is called 147 times; function 16 at $01108A, 67.

I also tried to split them into questions and orders by asking whether the script branches shortly after a call. It does not work: tested at every window from two instructions to six, the functions just drift from "almost never" into the middle, with no group at the other end. That is smearing, not signal, so the column is gone and the file says why. Telling a predicate from an action needs the VM's stack semantics, which are not decoded.
