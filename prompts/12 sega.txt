finalize sega deconstructing

****

Four things were still open in orig/sega/README.md. Three of them are
closed and the fourth is measurably smaller, and all of it came out of one
structural fix rather than four separate guesses.

The structural fix: switch tables.

A trace from the exception vectors stops dead at "jmp TBL(pc,Xn.w)",
because which case it takes is in a register. There are 47 of those in
this cartridge and none of them was being followed. But the table itself
is not a hole. It begins at the effective address the jmp's own
displacement names; it is either a run of word offsets from its own start
or a ladder of bra.s; and no case can jump backwards into it, so it ends
at the lowest address any of its own entries points at. The compiler also
puts a "cmpi.l #N,Dn" and a bgt in front, which says the same count a
second time, and where the two disagree the guard is right - three tables
read one entry too many on the lowest-target rule alone, and one of those
three swallowed two bytes of the routine's own code.

switch_tables() in tools/sega/sega_extract.py now reads all 47. That is
4074 bytes of new code and 1260 new instructions, and among what it opened
up were the two routines everything else here rests on.

The scenario loader, at $0161F0.

It is a switch on the record tag's high byte: eleven sections, one handler
each, and every handler says in 68000 how long its record is and what each
word in it means. The old reading was a pattern - look for runs of records
whose index counts 1, 2, 3 at a constant spacing - and it was wrong. It
had the reinforcements down as structures, because a map position's high
byte is $0A and so is the reinforcement tag, and it reported "3 structures
(10 bytes each)" for a mission that has no structures at all.

The new reading is the loader's own. The test is that it comes out even:
read straight through, record after record, and all 27 files land exactly
on their own $FFFF with nothing left over. missions.txt now has every unit
and building on the map with its health, the teams with their real
behaviour and movement names, the reinforcements with when they arrive and
whether they repeat, the spice blooms, the seed, and the starport's stock.
Each file is also written back out as the .INI it was converted from, in
res/data/files/SCEN*.ini.

Three things only the loader could settle. Section 5 is the Sardaukar, not
the Fremen - $0165AC maps a section number to a house through the table at
$0714A4, which holds 0, 1, 2, 4 - and they turn up in exactly three
missions, the ninth of each campaign, with 9000, 15500 and 10500 credits.
Section 6 is CHOAM, the starport's stock, keyed by unit index. And a
team's two letters go through the game's own lists, five behaviours at
$06CE66 and six movement types at $06CE4E; both searches take the first
match and there are two W's, so of Wheeled and Winged only Wheeled can
ever be named by a scenario file.

It also turned up a bug. SCENO009.INI is the last file in the directory,
so nothing follows it to say where it ends, and it was being cut 1212
bytes short by a hardcoded DATA_END. Parsing the records says where the
end is: the $FFFF is at $060E82 and the file is 2726 bytes.

The EMC virtual machine, at $016F40.

All nineteen opcodes are named now, and not out of anyone's general
knowledge of Westwood's VM - the interpreter is in the cartridge and it is
a 32-way switch with one handler each. Reading the handlers says what the
machine is: sixteen words of stack at +$16, a frame pointer at +$A, a
stack pointer at +$B counting down from 15, a return-value register at +8
and the script's variables at +$C. Opcode 16 is unary ! - ~ and opcode 17
is binary through a second 18-way switch at $0172A0: && || == != < <= > >=
+ - * / >> << & | % ^. Opcodes 19 to 31 all land on the same two
instructions, which stop the script, so nineteen is the whole set.

And the calling convention gives two more columns for free. Arguments are
pushed, the call is made, the caller drops them again - so the n in that
drop is the routine's arity, and a "push the return value" between the
call and the drop says the answer was wanted. That is the
question-from-order split the old functions.txt said could not be made
from the bytecode: 13 of the 79 routines the scripts actually call are
questions, the other 66 are orders, and when a routine answers it usually
answers at every one of its call sites - 7/7, 5/5, 4/4.

Bank 06, which was the largest open stretch.

$060E84 is read twice, at $004B7A and $004BB2, and both do the same four
instructions: lsl.w #3 for an eight-byte record, move.l $4(a1),$8(a0) for
the second longword whole, and andi.l #$FFFFFF on the first before it goes
to $FFBF04, which is where the graphics are read from. So a record is two
tagged 24-bit addresses - the pixels, and a list that goes with them - and
$00000000/$FFFFFFFF is an unused slot.

Nothing says how long the table is and nothing has to. Read forwards while
both halves are addresses inside the cartridge and it stops after 357
records, at $0619AC - and $0619AC is the lowest address any of those
records points at. The table ends exactly where the first thing it names
begins, which is not something a wrong length produces. Feeding its 222
pixel addresses and 188 list addresses through the same pointed-at pass
the code's own lea instructions get accounted for most of that bank; it is
no longer in the top twenty of gaps.txt.

Where the cartridge stands

                    at first    after the art    now
  code                14.9%          14.3%      14.7%
  named data          35.7%          78.2%      79.8%
  unaccounted for     49.4%           7.6%       5.5%

698 named regions, 59905 bytes still nobody's, down from 81427. The
rebuild is byte for byte against the original's SHA-256 at every step -
that was checked after each change, and the one time it broke it was a
shadowed variable in the emitter, not a bad reading.

What is still open: what each of the 104 EMC functions means, the picture
table's extra blocks at $0416DA, bank 04's static data segment, and the
remaining few-kilobyte stretches in banks 09 and 0C.
