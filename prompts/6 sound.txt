Nothing was lost - both ROMs still rebuilt byte for byte and every figure
from last time was still on disk - so I picked up the item that had been
sitting at the top of the "what's left" list in two files: the Mega Drive
sound driver's sequence format.

The sound looked dead, and I was wrong

The 68000 side is a C library of one function per driver command, and a
cross-reference scan over the whole cartridge said nothing calls any of
them except the initialiser - which installs four pointers to four FF
bytes. Read literally that means Dune II never plays a sound.

The scan was wrong, not the disassembler. The library is called with
jsr $1624.w - absolute short, 4EB8 - and I had only looked for 4EB9, 4EBA
and the branch forms. The listing had it right the whole time.

What settled it was measurement rather than more reading. I patched the
Genesis core to hand out the Z80's own 8 KB and gave retro-run a z80
command for it, then dumped that RAM at four points of the opening. The
command ring at $1B40 holds FF 0B and four live pointers into the sample
banks, and at the mentat it also holds FF 10 29, FF 12 00, FF 10 09. The
driver is busy; my scan was blind.

How the 68000 asks for a tune

Two bytes of Z80 RAM are the mailbox - a write index at $0036 the 68000
owns, a read index at $0037 the Z80 owns - over a 64-byte ring at $1B40. A
command is FF, an opcode and its arguments. There are 24 opcodes and the
game sends seven. The two that matter are $0B, which installs four 24-bit
addresses the driver reads everything through, and $10, which starts a
song by number. The game installs two sets: the front end's at $02DD14 and
the game's at $02DCC8.

What a song is

The third pointer is a directory of 16-bit offsets, little-endian inside a
big-endian cartridge. The song count is the first offset over two, because
song 0's header follows the last entry. A header is a track count and one
offset per track; each track gets one of the sixteen 32-byte voices and is
a byte stream:

  under $60   a note - the voice's sound slot says what it sounds like
  $60 to $72  nineteen commands: end, patch, volume, loop, jump, set,
              branch and the rest
  $80 to $BF  how long the note sounds, six bits a byte
  $C0 to $FF  how long until the next event, same encoding

13 songs in the front end's bank, 84 in the game's, 143 tracks between
them. New tool tools/sega/sega_seq.py, output in res/sound/music.txt with
a full event listing beside it, wired into make orig-sega.

Why the reading can be trusted

I made the tool check itself and refuse to finish if the check fails.
Follow every track to its end and add up the bytes: in both banks the
total is exactly the distance from the directory to the last track's last
byte - nothing claimed twice, nothing left over. And both banks stop where
the next resource pointer begins: the front end's songs end at $0D01F5 and
the game's at $0FE129, which are the very addresses command $0B installed
beside them. Neither of those falls out of a misread header.
