I started on the port's sound effects, which is what I had said was
unblocked, and the deconstruction answered the question instead of solving
it. Then I took the cartridge from 13.3% unaccounted to 7.6%.

The sound effects: the answer is no, and here is why

The plan was to lift the Mega Drive's effects as samples. First I needed
their rate, and that came out of the code: a sample's flags byte carries a
YM2612 timer A period in its low nibble - the driver at $01370 writes
registers $24 and $25 with the negated nibble - so one byte leaves the DAC
every k ticks and the rate is 53267 Hz over k. Four values are in use: 5,
7, 8 and 10, so 10653, 7610, 6658 and 5327 Hz.

The emulator agrees, which is the point of saying it. Dumping the Z80's
8 KB frame by frame and watching the stream pointer at $02FC, a nibble of 5
consumes about 202 bytes a frame and a nibble of 10 about 106 - 10.0 and
5.3 kHz at 49.7 frames a second. The two-to-one spread I had first read as
measurement noise turned out to be the format.

Then I found the game's own sound list: 58 names of 16 bytes at $020870,
eighteen tunes and then the effects and the speech. The code proves the
numbering - moveq #25 before the sound dispatcher is "credit down", moveq
#40 is "explosion 1", and that one appears at thirteen call sites.

And that settled it. A note is only a pitch; what makes the noise is the
instrument the track's patch names, and type 1 is the sampled one, taking
YM2612 channel 6 which is the DAC. Reading every song that way:

  positive select, invalid select, building, rifle (fx),
  machinegun (fx)                                        PSG noise
  placement, countdown, credit down, credit up, rifle,
  machinegun, explosion 1                                FM
  songs 64-83, the twenty unnamed ones                   sampled

The Mega Drive's gunfire and explosions are synthesised. Only its speech is
sampled. There is no sampled explosion in this cartridge to lift, so the
port's six effects stay with the AY - not because no clean recording
exists, which is what the docs said, but because the sound does not exist
as a sample in either original.

What the port can have instead is speech: 30 clips, extracted at their real
rates by sega_seq.py --wav into res/sound/samples/. I have not named them
individually. Nothing in the cartridge names them and telling "yes sir"
from "acknowledged" needs an ear, not a measurement.

The cartridge: 13.3% unaccounted to 7.6%

Four things did it.

The picture table at $0416DA. 36 longwords in groups ended by a zero, each
group an asset list and then the Format80 blocks that go with it. CLAUDE.md
already knew this table named two asset lists; it names fourteen more
things besides. The blocks check each other - $03246A's stream ends at
exactly $032E56, the next entry - so the extents are exact. What is in them
is still open: not whole tiles, and not tilemaps either, since both were
tested and both fail.

A pointed-at pass. Anything still nobody's that a traced instruction builds
the address of - lea $60E84.l,a1 is not a guess - now gets a banner saying
who reads it. That is 40 KB, and it is a weaker claim than the rest, so the
tool counts it apart and says so on its own line rather than burying it in
the total.

The sound name tables, and the 29 names of the PC Dune II's own music files
kept whole beside them.

And the sweep fix from the last round kept paying: two more name tables it
had been reading as instructions.

Where it stands

  code                14.3%
  named data          78.2%   (3.9% of it only pointed at)
  unaccounted          7.6%
